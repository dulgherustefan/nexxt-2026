# Codifiable rules for Romanian bank back-office processes: KYC/AML, credit underwriting, payments/fraud, complaint handling, data protection (status checked 28 Oct 2026)

*Verification tags used below: **[P]** = read in the primary/official text during this session (legislatie.just.ro consolidated text, official PDF, ECB page); **[S]** = secondary source (law firm, press, bank page), not checked against the primary text; **[U]** = unverified, conflicting or draft. Romanian legal terms are kept in Romanian. "Consolidated" dates are the latest consolidation dates shown on legislatie.just.ro when the text was downloaded on 28 Oct 2026.*

## 1. KYC/AML: Legea 129/2019, Regulamentul BNR 2/2019, beneficial owners/RBE, PEPs, sanctions, ONPCSB reporting, EU AML package

### Takeaway
Romanian KYC/AML is highly codifiable: Legea 129/2019 (consolidated 19.12.2025) fixes the CDD triggers (relationship; occasional ≥15,000 EUR; transfer of funds ≥1,000 EUR; any suspicion), the BO indicator (25% + 1 share / >25%), PEP lists and EDD measures, the STR mechanics (report before executing, 24h hold, ONPCSB suspension up to 48h + 72h) and threshold reports (cash and external transfers ≥10,000 EUR within 3 working days), while Regulamentul BNR 2/2019 lists the exact data fields to collect and leaves refresh frequency to risk-based internal norms. The big pending change is the EU package: AMLR applies from 10 July 2027 (refresh ≤1 year for high risk / ≤5 years otherwise, BO "25% or more", occasional-transaction CDD at 10,000 EUR, 10,000 EUR cash cap) and AMLA starts direct supervision of selected groups from 2028.

#### CDD triggers and levels (Legea 129/2019)
- Art. 13(1) [P]: standard CDD ("măsuri standard de cunoaștere a clientelei") is mandatory (a) when establishing a business relationship; (b) for occasional transactions of at least the lei equivalent of 15,000 EUR (single or linked operations) or an occasional transfer of funds (as defined in Regulation (EU) 2023/1113) of at least 1,000 EUR; (c) for traders in goods making occasional cash transactions ≥10,000 EUR; (d) whenever there is ML/TF suspicion, regardless of amount or exemptions; (e) when there are doubts about previously obtained identification data. Other thresholds: gambling ≥2,000 EUR (art. 13(2)); exchange offices ≥2,000 EUR (13(2^1)); crypto-asset service providers ≥1,000 EUR (13(2^2)) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Art. 11(8) [P]: identity of the client and beneficial owner must be verified before establishing the relationship or performing the occasional transaction; art. 11(8^1): obtain proof of BO registration or an extract from the central BO registers whenever starting a relationship with an entity subject to BO registration; art. 11(3): risk-based systems to determine whether the client or its BO is a PEP; art. 11(4): verify that a person acting on behalf of the client is authorised and identify/verify that person — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Art. 14 [P] (as amended by Legea 86/2025): CDD applies to all new clients and to existing clients based on risk and materiality, when relevant circumstances change, or when the entity must contact the client during the calendar year to review BO information (incl. obligations under Legea 207/2015 art. 62, tax-information exchange) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272); [Legea 86/2025](https://legislatie.just.ro/public/DetaliiDocument/298293)
- Simplified CDD, art. 16 [P]: only for clients rated low risk after a global risk assessment; low-risk indicators include listed companies subject to disclosure rules, public administrations/enterprises, low-risk geographies (EU member states, third countries with effective AML/CFT systems or low corruption), life-insurance policies with annual premium ≤1,000 EUR or single premium ≤2,500 EUR, pension products without early surrender, financial-inclusion products. Identification/verification of client and BO and transaction monitoring are still always required (16(4)); SDD is prohibited whenever there is ML/TF suspicion (16(5)) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Enhanced CDD, art. 17(1) [P]: mandatory for (a) countries that do not/insufficiently apply AML standards or are non-cooperative; (b) correspondent relationships with credit/financial institutions; (c) PEPs or clients whose BO is a PEP, "inclusiv pentru o perioadă de cel puțin 12 luni" after the person left the function; (d) natural/legal persons established in high-risk third countries identified by the European Commission; (e) cases set by sectoral rules. Art. 17(2): examine context and purpose of transactions that are complex, unusually large, outside the usual pattern, or without apparent economic/lawful purpose — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Art. 17^1(1) [P]: for EC-listed high-risk third countries, obtain additional information on client and BO, on the intended relationship, on source of funds and source of wealth (client and BO), on the reasons for transactions; senior management approval; enhanced monitoring (more controls, selection of transaction types for extra review) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Art. 17(14) [P] higher-risk factors to code in a risk score: unusual circumstances; residence in high-risk areas; personal asset-holding vehicles; nominee shareholders or bearer shares; cash-intensive businesses; unusually complex ownership; investor-citizenship/residence schemes; private banking; anonymity-favouring products; non-face-to-face relationships without safeguards such as eIDAS electronic identification or a remote identification process "reglementat, recunoscut, aprobat sau acceptat de către Autoritatea pentru Digitalizarea României"; payments from unknown/unassociated third parties; new products/technologies; trade in oil, arms, precious metals, tobacco, cultural goods, ivory/protected species; countries under EU/UN sanctions or embargoes; countries supporting terrorism — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Art. 18(1) of Regulamentul BNR 2/2019 [P]: institutions must not start or continue a business relationship or perform an occasional transaction if they cannot apply CDD (including when they cannot establish the legitimacy of purpose/nature or manage the risk); art. 18(2) covers relationships that cannot be terminated (e.g., an outstanding loan) — [Regulamentul BNR 2/2019](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/217844)

#### Required data and documents (Regulamentul BNR 2/2019)
- Individuals, art. 8(1) [P]: name, surname and pseudonym; date and place of birth; CNP or similar unique identifier; address and its legal regime (domiciliu/reședință); citizenship; occupation and employer name or nature of own activity; phone/fax/e-mail if any; purpose and nature of the relationship; source of funds to be used; PEP / PEP-family / close-associate status; BO information if different from the client. For occasional transactions (art. 8(2)) a reduced set applies (a-e, j, k). BO minimum data (art. 8(3)): nature of the relationship generating BO status, name, date/place of birth, CNP or equivalent, country of residence, citizenship, nature of activity, PEP status — [Regulamentul BNR 2/2019](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/217844)
- Representatives, art. 9 [P]: for clients acting through a legal representative, proxy, curator, guardian etc., obtain and verify the representative's identity (name, date/place of birth, CNP, address/regime, citizenship) and the nature and limits of the representation — [Regulamentul BNR 2/2019](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/217844)
- Legal entities, art. 10(1) [P]: name; legal form; tax ID and/or trade register number (or foreign equivalent) and LEI code if any; registered office and, where applicable, real seat; contact details; type and nature of activity; identification data of the representatives and their powers to bind the entity; purpose and nature of the relationship; BO information. Legea 129/2019 art. 15(1^1)-(2) adds: ask for the real seat if different from the registered office; for foreign legal persons, a legalised Romanian translation of constitutive/registration documents [P] — [Regulamentul BNR 2/2019](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/217844); [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Verification sources, art. 11 [P]: documents "din categoria celor mai greu de falsificat", e.g., identity documents with a photo issued by an official authority; constitutive documents registered with the trade register; or the electronic identification means provided by Legea 129/2019; other data can be verified from credible independent sources such as DGEP (population register), IGI (immigration), ONRC databases, other public authorities, private databases fed by public authorities, operating licences, audit reports, tax documents, bank statements. Art. 12: BO data for legal entities verified against the BO registers of art. 19(5) Legea 129/2019 (or foreign equivalents) — [Regulamentul BNR 2/2019](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/217844)
- Remote (video) onboarding: a bank notice states remote video identification relies on the KYC rules of Regulamentul BNR 2/2019 together with eIDAS Regulation (EU) 910/2014 and requires explicit consent for biometric data [S] — [Libra Bank notice](https://www.librabank.ro/documente/formulare/informare-prelucrare-date-personale-proces-identificare-la-distanta.pdf)

#### Periodic KYC refresh
- Romania today: no fixed legal interval. Regulamentul BNR 2/2019 art. 5(2)(e) requires internal norms to set "frecvența actualizării periodice, pe bază de risc, a informațiilor și a documentației despre clienți" plus event-driven updates; art. 13(1)-(2): periodic verification of the truthfulness and adequacy of client/BO data proportional to risk, and risk-profile updates when client information, products offered or identified risks change [P] — [Regulamentul BNR 2/2019](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/217844)
- From 10 July 2027, AMLR art. 26(2) sets outer limits: client information must be updated at least once a year for higher-risk customers and at least every five years for all others (ceilings, not default cycles); art. 26(3) adds event-driven triggers (change in relevant circumstances, legal obligation to contact the client about BO, awareness of a relevant fact) [S] — [Didit, AMLR art. 26](https://didit.me/blog/eu-amlr-ongoing-monitoring-article-26/); [CMS, AMLA draft guidelines under art. 26(5)](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/amla-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-ongoing-monitoring-under-article-26-5-amlr)
- AMLA consulted on draft ongoing-monitoring guidelines (art. 26(5) AMLR) from 3 June 2026 to 3 September 2026; final status not confirmed [S] — [Accountancy Europe response, Sept 2026](https://accountancyeurope.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/260902-Accountancy-Europe-responds-to-AMLAs-consultation-on-ongoing-monitoring-guidelines.pdf); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/amla-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-ongoing-monitoring-under-article-26-5-amlr)
- Operational pain point: Romanian banks run data-update campaigns citing Legea 129/2019 and Regulamentul BNR 2/2019 and warn that accounts may be restricted or closed if clients do not update data [S] — [Profit.ro](https://profit.ro/povesti-cu-profit/financiar/banci/bancile-avertizeaza-clientii-ca-trebuie-sa-actualizeze-datele-daca-nu-vor-sa-le-fie-inchise-conturile-20067913); in 2026 press reports describe restrictions applied automatically by bank systems after the update deadline, with clients often discovering the block at payment or ATM [S] — [Capital, May 2026](https://www.capital.ro/multi-romani-risca-sa-ramana-fara-acces-la-banii-din-cont-banca-trimite-notificari-verificarile-pe-care-clientii-trebuie-sa-le-faca.html); [Playtech, 2026](https://playtech.ro/2026/multi-romani-pot-ramane-fara-acces-la-banii-din-cont-daca-ignora-aceste-detalii-situatiile-in-care-cardul-poate-sa-nu-mai-functioneze/); a bank may ask for updates at any time, even without a change in client behaviour [S] — [HotNews](https://hotnews.ro/cunoasterea-clientelei-episodul-1-de-ce-trebuie-sa-ne-actualizam-datele-la-banci-si-ct-de-des-trebuie-sa-facem-asta-232467)

#### Beneficial owners and the RBE at ONRC
- Definition, art. 4(2)(a) Legea 129/2019 [P]: BO of a company = natural person(s) who ultimately own or control it through a sufficient percentage of shares/voting rights/equity or control by other means (listed companies excepted). "Deținerea a 25% plus unu acțiuni sau participare în capital ... într-un procent de peste 25%" by a natural person = indicator of direct ownership; the same held via foreign corporate entities controlled by the same natural person = indicator of indirect ownership. Fallback (pt. 2): if no BO can be identified after all diligence (and no suspicion), or there is doubt, the senior managing official (administrator(s), board members, directors with delegated powers, directorate members) is recorded, and the entity documents the steps and difficulties — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Art. 19 [P]: legal persons must hold adequate, accurate, current BO information and provide it to reporting entities when CDD is applied (19(3)); BO data for entities registered in the trade register are recorded in a central register organised at ONRC (19(5)(a)); authorities with access must report discrepancies between the central registers and their own BO information (19(7)); art. 19(8)-(9) access provisions were amended by Legea 86/2025 — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Filing frequency [S/U, conflicting]: a 2026 practitioner guide says the BO declaration is filed at registration and within 15 days of any change; an annual declaration remains only for companies with shareholders registered/with tax seat in non-cooperative jurisdictions (within 15 days after approval of annual accounts) — [bono.ro](https://bono.ro/blog/declaratie-beneficiar-real); one source attributes removal of the annual declaration to Legea 315/2021 (applicable from 1 Jan 2022), bono.ro attributes it to OUG 123/2022, and Profit.ro reported it being reinstated and then removed again — [Fiscalitatea](https://www.fiscalitatea.ro/declaratia-privind-beneficiarii-reali-nu-mai-trebuie-depusa-anual-insa-anumite-persoane-au-in-continuare-aceasta-obligatielegea-315221-21596/); [Profit.ro (removed again)](https://profit.ro/taxe-si-consultanta/declaratia-anuala-privind-beneficiarul-real-este-eliminata-din-nou-exceptii-si-noi-conditii-la-declararea-beneficiarului-20496008)
- AMLR (from 10 July 2027) art. 52: BO via ownership = "25 % or more" (inclusive; exactly 25% qualifies, unlike Romania's current ">25%" indicator); indirect stakes = multiply percentages along each chain and sum across chains; by 10 July 2029 the Commission may set lower thresholds (down to 15%) for higher-risk entity categories [S] — [Springlex, AMLR art. 52](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/amlr-regulation/article-52/)

#### PEPs
- Art. 3(2) Legea 129/2019 [P]: important public functions = heads of state/government, ministers, deputy ministers or secretaries of state; members of Parliament or similar legislative bodies; members of governing bodies of political parties; judges of supreme/constitutional/high courts whose decisions are subject only to extraordinary appeal; members of governing bodies of courts of auditors or central bank boards; ambassadors, chargés d'affaires and senior armed-forces officers; board/supervisory board members and managers of regii autonome, majority state-owned companies and national companies; directors, deputy directors and board members of international organisations. Intermediate/junior positions excluded (3(3)). ANI (Agenția Națională de Integritate) compiles and publishes the list of important public functions, updated within 5 days of changes (3(3^1)-(3^3)) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Family members (3(4)): spouse/partner; children and their spouses/partners; parents. Close associates (3(5)): joint BO with a PEP or other close business relationship; sole BO of an entity set up for the de facto benefit of a PEP [P] — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- End of PEP status, art. 3(6) as amended by Legea 86/2025 (in force 26.05.2025) [P]: one year after leaving the function the person is no longer treated as a PEP, unless he/she still presents PEP-associated risk because of residual influence, in which case the period is extended by one more year. (Art. 17(1)(c) still requires EDD for "at least 12 months".) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- PEP EDD measures, art. 17(9) [P]: senior management approval to establish or continue the relationship; adequate measures to establish source of wealth and source of funds (the rest of the paragraph was truncated in the extract read) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)

#### Reporting to ONPCSB (FIU)
- STR (raport pentru tranzacții suspecte), art. 6(1) [P]: reported exclusively to ONPCSB when the entity knows, suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that assets derive from crime or relate to TF, or that the information it holds may be useful for enforcing the law; also when ONPCSB asks the entity to consider a future transaction suspicious (6(3)) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- STR timing and holds, art. 8 [P]: (1) send "de îndată" and before executing any client transaction linked to the suspicion; (2) ONPCSB confirms immediately by allocating a registration number; (3) the transaction is not executed for 24 hours from registration, after which it may proceed if ONPCSB has not ordered suspension; (4) ONPCSB may suspend for up to 48 hours; (6) ONPCSB may ask the Prosecutor's Office at the High Court (PÎCCJ) once for an extension of up to 72 hours; (9) if no extension is communicated before expiry, the entity may execute; (10) hour-based deadlines falling on a non-working day move to the same hour of the next working day; (12) reports only electronically via ONPCSB channels; (13)-(14) non-compliant reports are returned and must be corrected within 2 working days (the 24h clock restarts on confirmation of the corrected STR) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Threshold reports, art. 7 [P]: cash transactions in lei or foreign currency ≥ lei equivalent of 10,000 EUR, including linked operations (7(1); reported by the credit/financial institution through which they pass, 7(2)); external transfers into and out of accounts ≥10,000 EUR equivalent, including linked operations (7(3)); money-remittance transfers ≥2,000 EUR (7(5)); deadline: "în cel mult 3 zile lucrătoare de la momentul efectuării tranzacției" (7(7)) — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)
- Report formats: ONPCSB Order 191/2023 (MO 625/10.07.2023) approved the form/content of the art. 6 and 7 reports; its application was postponed to 22 November 2023 by Order 261/2023 [P] — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated (editorial notes)](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272); new templates in use since 22 Nov 2023 [S] — [Avocatnet](https://avocatnet.ro/t17776/raport-tranzactii-suspecte.html)
- Record retention, art. 21 [P]: CDD records (ID copies, monitoring and verification results, eID/trust-service evidence) and transaction records (account files, commercial correspondence, results of analyses of complex/unusually large transactions) kept 5 years from the end of the business relationship or from the occasional transaction — [Legea 129/2019 consolidated](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/305272)

#### Sanctions screening and asset freezing (OUG 202/2008)

#### EU AML package timeline (Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 AMLR, Directive (EU) 2024/1640 AMLD6, Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 AMLA)
- AMLR applies directly from 10 July 2027; AMLD6 general transposition deadline 10 July 2027 [S] — [wiki.private.law](https://wiki.private.law/en/eu-aml-package); [Didit, AMLR art. 26](https://didit.me/blog/eu-amlr-ongoing-monitoring-article-26/)
- AMLA: first selection of directly supervised entities runs for six months from 1 July 2027, with direct supervision of up to ~40 groups from 1 January 2028; criteria: activity in at least six member states plus high residual risk [S] — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/07/direct-supervisory-powers-of-the-new-european-antimoney-laundering-authority); [wiki.private.law](https://wiki.private.law/en/eu-aml-package); conflicting: Mondaq says direct supervision expected "by July 2028" [U] — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/money-laundering/1658810/direct-supervisory-powers-of-the-new-european-anti-money-laundering-authority)
- AMLR art. 69: obliged entities report suspicions on their own initiative regardless of amount (including attempted transactions and cases where CDD cannot be completed) and must answer FIU information requests within 5 working days; the FIU may shorten this to under 24 hours in justified urgent cases or extend it [S] — [Springlex, AMLR art. 69](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/amlr-regulation/article-69/); art. 71 (refraining from transactions; secondary sources cite a 3-working-day FIU response window) [U] — same source

### Inferences
- An AML agent can implement a deterministic first layer: (1) CDD-level selector (standard / simplified only if low-risk and no suspicion / enhanced if PEP, EC high-risk country, correspondent, art. 17(14) factors); (2) required-field completeness check against BNR Reg 2/2019 art. 8-10; (3) BO computation (multiply-and-sum chains; flag >25% now and ≥25% from 10 July 2027; fallback to senior managing official with documented diligence); (4) RBE cross-check and discrepancy flag (art. 11(8^1), 19(7)); (5) report generators with SLA timers (STR immediately and before execution with 24h/48h/72h hold logic; RTN/RTE within 3 working days; corrections within 2 working days).
- Because Romanian refresh frequency is set by internal norms, a hackathon prototype should parameterise refresh intervals by risk class and pre-configure the AMLR ceilings (≤1 year high risk, ≤5 years others) as the 2027 default.
- Threshold rules that differ between today and July 2027 (15,000 vs 10,000 EUR occasional CDD; ">25%" vs "≥25%" BO) should be versioned by effective date in the rule base, not hard-coded.
- For a bank, a confirmed sanctions hit triggers: freeze without delay, immediate report to BNR as national competent authority (OUG 202/2008 art. 12(1)(o), 18, 24), any derogation/authorisation routed to ANAF (art. 12(1)(p)), and possibly an STR to ONPCSB if there is ML/TF suspicion; "flag exception + human confirmation of the match" is the correct agent output rather than an automated decision, because name-matching false positives are common.

## 2. Credit underwriting: Regulamentul BNR 17/2012, consumer credit law (OUG 50/2010, CCD2), state programmes, documents and rejection reasons

### Takeaway
Regulamentul BNR 17/2012 (consolidated 25.10.2022) gives hard, codifiable limits: DSTI ≤40% (45% for a first home for someone who never owned one), FX exposure for unhedged borrowers ≤20% of income (25% first home), LTV caps of 85% (lei) / 80% (FX income) / 75% (EUR unhedged) / 60% (other FX), minus 10 pp if the applicant already owns a home, state-guaranteed loans exempt, consumer loans ≤5 years, plus a 15%-of-volume exception quota (not for loans >250,000 EUR or borrowers below the average net wage). No numeric stress add-ons remain in the regulation; a 2026 BNR draft would add securities-backed (Lombard) loans exempt from DSTI. CCD2 had to be applied from 20 Nov 2026, but no Romanian transposition act was found as of late Oct 2026, so OUG 50/2010 remains the operative consumer-credit law.

#### Regulamentul BNR 17/2012 privind unele condiții de creditare (consolidated 25.10.2022; amended by Reg. 6/2018, Reg. 5/2020, Reg. 3/2022 and later 2022 changes)

- Income rules, art. 15 [P]: eligible current income may not exceed the previous year's by more than 20%; previous-year income is established from documents certifying income declared to tax authorities and/or income received in accounts at the lender; higher income only with documents proving continuity (promotion, new job, business growth for self-employed). - Creditworthiness assessment, art. 11^1-11^4 [P]: rigorous assessment before contract and on any increase of the loan amount (re-assessment not needed if the original assessment covered the increase and data are ≤30 days old); joint applicants assessed on aggregate capacity; credit decision must be clear, documented, with a maximum validity period, after which the process restarts; information underlying approval kept at least for the loan term, with an income-history record and verification steps available immediately to BNR; assessment based predominantly on the client's ability to generate income, collateral only secondary; data obtained from clients and internal/external sources incl. credit-bureau-type databases and credit intermediaries, with reasonable verification including independent sources — [Reg. 17/2012](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/143876)
- FX information duty, art. 7-9 [P]: unhedged FX applicants must receive, on paper or durable medium at least 15 calendar days before contracting (reducible at the applicant's written request), a personalised illustration of payments under a severe depreciation of the leu (and, for non-fixed rates, combined with rate increases) — [Reg. 17/2012](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/143876)
- Exemptions from the DSTI/LTV chapters, art. 4(1) [P]: loans for studies, medical or funeral expenses; loans fully cash-collateralised at the lender (for the covered amount); restructurings at the same lender for financial difficulty; loans repaid from subsidies; restructurings under state facility laws; conversions of FX loans into lei or into the currency of income; take-over of loans on death of the debtor or asset partition. ## 3. Payments and fraud: Legea 209/2019 (PSD2), SCA, chargebacks, instant payments and VoP (Regulation (EU) 2024/886), PSR/PSD3, fraud statistics

### Takeaway
Legea 209/2019 gives precise, codifiable consumer-protection rules: notify unauthorised/incorrect transactions within 13 months; PSP refunds by the end of the next business day unless it has reasonable grounds to suspect fraud and notifies the authority in writing; payer liability capped at 30 EUR (stricter than PSD2), zero if no SCA was required, full liability only for fraud or intentional/grossly negligent breach (burden of proof on the PSP); direct-debit refunds within 8 weeks with a 10-business-day answer. For Romania (non-euro), the Instant Payments Regulation applies from 9 Jan 2027 (receive euro instant payments, price parity), 9 July 2027 (send, VoP), 9 June 2028 (outside business hours, national-currency accounts). PSR/PSD3 was politically agreed in Nov 2025 but not yet in force; fraud is shifting to social engineering (vishing 42% of reports in H1 2026), which current PSD2 refund rules largely do not cover.

- Art. 169 [P]: the PSP corrects an unauthorised or incorrectly executed transaction only if the user notifies without undue delay and no later than 13 months after the debit date; the 13-month limit does not apply if the PSP failed to provide the required transaction information — [Legea 209/2019 original text (CSALB copy)](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)

- Art. 173 [P]: for an unauthorised transaction the payer's PSP refunds "imediat sau cel târziu la sfârșitul următoarei zile lucrătoare" after noting or being notified, restores the account and ensures the value date is not later than the debit date, except where it has reasonable grounds to suspect fraud and communicates those grounds in writing to the national competent authority; art. 174: same next-business-day refund when a PISP was involved — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Art. 177 [P]: a payer who did not act fraudulently bears no loss where (a) loss/theft/misuse could not be detected before payment; (b) loss caused by PSP staff, agents, branches or outsourcees; (c) after notifying loss/theft under art. 166(1)(b); (d) the payer's PSP does not require strong customer authentication; (e) the PSP did not provide means to notify loss/theft at any time — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Art. 179 [P]: (1) the payer may bear losses up to "cel mult 30 euro sau echivalentul în lei" from a lost, stolen or misappropriated instrument if not fraudulent and no intentional breach of art. 166 obligations; (2) the payer bears all losses caused by fraud or by intentional or grossly negligent breach of art. 166 obligations (use instrument per terms; notify loss/theft without undue delay; keep personalised security credentials safe); (3) assessment considers the nature of the security credentials and the circumstances — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Art. 178 [P]: if the payee or payee's PSP does not accept SCA, it must refund the financial damage caused to the payer's PSP — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- SCA, art. 220 [P]: PSPs apply SCA when the payer accesses the payment account online, initiates an electronic payment, or carries out any action through a remote channel that may imply a risk of fraud; remote electronic payments require dynamic linking to a specific amount and payee; SCA = two or more independent elements of knowledge, possession and inherence (definitions article) — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Card pre-authorisation, art. 180-181 [P]: funds can be blocked only with consent to the exact amount, and released without undue delay once the exact amount is known and at the latest immediately after receipt of the payment order — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Direct-debit/payee-initiated refunds, art. 182-185 [P]: refund request within 8 weeks of the debit; within 10 business days the PSP refunds the full amount or justifies refusal, indicating the bodies the payer can refer to (art. 222); no right to refuse for direct debits under art. 182(5) — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Execution time, art. 195 [P]: amount credited to the payee's PSP by the end of the next business day after receipt of the order (+1 business day for paper-initiated orders) — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Authorised push-payment scams: a consumer guide explains that refunds under the law target unauthorised transactions; where the client authorised the payment under manipulation, the automatic refund regime does not apply in the same way, and exceptions exist where the PSP suspects client fraud [S] — [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/ro-ro/blog/hotforsecurity/frauda-autorizata-vs-neautorizata)

### Inferences
- An unauthorised-transaction triage agent can apply: (1) is the claim within 13 months of debit (or was information not provided)?; (2) was SCA required and applied?; (3) any evidence of fraud by the payer → if "reasonable grounds", written notice to the competent authority and hold, else refund by end of next business day; (4) liability split: 0 EUR (art. 177 cases), up to 30 EUR (art. 179(1)), full (fraud/intent/gross negligence proven by the PSP).
- Authorised push-payment (vishing/investment) scams fall outside the unauthorised-refund regime until PSR applies (impersonation of PSP staff only, ~2028), so the agent should classify "authorised vs unauthorised" first; this is where most Romanian fraud growth is (vishing 42% of H1 2026 reports).
- For Romania the instant-payment and VoP deadlines are 2027, giving a concrete compliance driver for name-matching and sanctions-screening automation (daily list re-screening of the client base).
- Chargeback deadlines are scheme-driven (≈120 days) and should be configured per scheme/reason code rather than hard-coded from law.

## 4. Complaint handling: statutory deadlines, BNR/ANPC roles, CSALB procedure and volumes, EBA/ESMA guidelines

### Takeaway
Two statutory clocks matter: payment-service complaints must be answered within 15 business days, extendable only in exceptional cases (with a holding reply) up to 35 business days (Legea 209/2019 art. 224), and consumer-credit complaints within 30 days of registration (OUG 50/2010 art. 42, enforced by ANPC). Banks must inform clients about ANPC, BNR and the ADR body; the ADR body is CSALB (free, ≤90 days, settlement with the force of a court decision), whose volumes jumped 60% in 2026 (3,394 requests in 9M 2026) with banks settling ~90% of negotiated files while IFNs accept almost none.

### Cited Findings
- Payment services, Legea 209/2019 art. 224 [P]: PSPs reply on paper or another agreed durable medium, addressing all points, "nu mai târziu de 15 zile lucrătoare de la data primirii plângerii"; in exceptional situations beyond the PSP's control, a holding reply must state the reasons for the delay and the date of the final answer, which may not exceed 35 business days from receipt — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Art. 225 [P]: PSPs must inform users of their right to refer disputes to ANPC, ANAF, BNR and the competent ADR entity under Legea 192/2006 (mediation) or OG 38/2015 (ADR), clearly on the website, in branches and in the general terms — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Direct-debit refund refusals must indicate the bodies the payer can refer to (art. 185(2)) [P] — [Legea 209/2019](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/legea-nr-209-2019-privind-serviciile-de-plata-si-pentru-modificarea-unor-acte-normative.pdf)
- Consumer credit, OUG 50/2010 art. 42: creditors must receive and register consumer complaints and take all measures to answer within maximum 30 days of registration and remedy the harm [S] — [notari.pro, art. 42](https://notari.pro/oug-50-2010-contractele-de-credit/art-42-informatii-si-drepturi-privind-contractele-de-credit); ANPC lists failure to answer within 30 days among sanctioned breaches [S] — [ANPC press release (InfoTVA)](https://infotva.manager.ro/articole/infotva/comunicat-de-presa-anpc-ref-contractele-de-credit-3283.html)
- Bank practice examples [S]: BCR policy: 30 days from registration, extendable by at most 15 days in justified cases; payment-service complaints final answer within 35 business days — [BCR complaint-process note](https://cdn0.erstegroup.com/content/dam/ro/bcr/www_bcr_ro/Contact/Documente/PRECIZARI-PRIVIND-PROCESUL-DE-ADMINISTRARE-A-RECLAMATIILOR.pdf); BNP Paribas Romania: written acknowledgement within 2 business days, answer within 15 business days with exceptions — [BNP Paribas RO](https://www.romania.bnpparibas.com/app/uploads/sites/37/2022/11/complaint-process-ro.pdf)
- EBA/ESMA Joint Committee Guidelines on complaints-handling (JC 2014 43, updated as JC 2018 35) set expectations on complaints-management policy and function, registration, reporting, internal follow-up, information to complainants and response procedures; competent authorities must notify compliance; extended by EBA to credit servicers under Directive (EU) 2021/2167 [S] — [JC 2018 35 (RO)](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/2381463/8e2247ee-c9b2-42c3-9eab-3fe0e32e254c/Joint%20Committee%20Guidelines%20on%20complaints-handling%20%28JC%202018%2035%29_RO.pdf); [EBA hearing on credit servicers](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-01/f9abf047-d7fb-4c19-8810-66171bca08ae/EBA%20public%20hearing%20-%20GLs%20on%20complaints%20handling%20by%20credit%20servicers%20under%20CSD_Final.pdf)
- CSALB procedure [S/P]: free of charge; negotiation mediated "în mai puțin de trei luni" for contracts in progress; request via online form; if the bank accepts, a conciliator is appointed (16 conciliators); the parties' agreement has "puterea unei hotărâri în instanță"; requests to delete Biroul de Credit records cannot be negotiated through CSALB (resolved directly by the parties or closed) — [CSALB press release, 20 Oct 2026](https://alba24.ro/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Crestere-cereri_Comunicat-de-presa-CSALB_20.10.2026.pdf); the conciliator must be accepted by both parties and the procedure lasts at most 90 days (legal limit); average duration 13 days in a prior year; files are closed if the consumer does not respond within 90 days [S] — [CSALB, Feb 2023](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Timp-de-negociere_Comunicat-CSALB_28.02.2023.pdf); [CSALB report Q3 2025](https://csalb.ro/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Raport-CSALB_T3-2025_RO_29.10.2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- A complaint-triage agent needs at least three SLA clocks: payment-service complaints (15 business days, hard stop 35 business days with mandatory holding letter), consumer-credit complaints (30 calendar days from registration), and direct-debit refund requests (10 business days); classification by product and legal basis is therefore the first step.

- The 60% rise in CSALB requests in 2026 (payment difficulties) indicates a growing restructuring/renegotiation workload that an agent could pre-screen (affordability recalculation using Reg. 17/2012 metrics).

## 5. Data protection specific to bank operations: banking secrecy, GDPR art. 22 (automated decisions) in credit and KYC, credit bureau, AI Act

### Takeaway
Banking secrecy (OUG 99/2006 art. 111 ff.) and GDPR both constrain agent design: CJEU SCHUFA (C-634/21) and Dun & Bradstreet (C-203/22) make credit scores that decisively drive a lending decision "automated decisions" under GDPR art. 22 with a right to a meaningful explanation; CCD2 art. 18 adds a right to human intervention in automated creditworthiness assessments; and creditworthiness AI is a high-risk use case under the AI Act, whose Annex III obligations were postponed to 2 Dec 2027 by the Digital Omnibus (Council approval 29 June 2026). Practical rules: consent-based ANAF income queries, Biroul de Credit reporting only after 30 days overdue with 15 days' notice, and 5-year AML record retention.

### Cited Findings
- Banking secrecy [S]: OUG 99/2006 art. 111 protects facts, data and information on the institution's activity and its clients, where "client" includes anyone with whom the institution negotiated a transaction even if not concluded and any present or past user of its services; restrictions extend to persons obtaining the information from the institution's reports/documents (art. 112); staff may not use confidential information for their own or others' benefit (art. 113); disclosure is permitted, e.g., with prosecutor authorisation (art. 114) and for consolidated supervision and AML/CFT purposes (art. 115) — [Legeaz, "secret bancar"](https://legeaz.net/dictionar-juridic/secret-bancar)
- GDPR art. 22 and credit scoring [S]: CJEU C-634/21 SCHUFA (7 Dec 2023): calculating a credit score is an automated individual decision under art. 22 GDPR where a third party "draws strongly" on that score to establish, implement or terminate a contract; the Court rejected the argument that scoring is merely preparatory, citing the risk of circumventing art. 22 and a gap in protection — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/cjeu-rules-that-a-credit-score-constitutes-automated-decision-making-under-the-gdpr); [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2023/global/key-takeaways-from-the-schufa-case-of-the-cjeu); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/key-takeaways-from-the-cjeus-recent-automated-decision-making-rulings)

### Inferences
- Given SCHUFA's anti-circumvention reasoning, a nominal human sign-off that simply rubber-stamps an agent's score is unlikely to take the decision outside art. 22; human reviewers need real authority and information to depart from the recommendation.
- For a hackathon agent, the safe pattern is "decision support, human decides" for any adverse credit or KYC outcome: the agent validates, scores and flags, but a qualified human takes the final decision with reasons recorded, which addresses GDPR art. 22, CCD2 art. 18 and future AI Act duties at once.
- Explanations should be rule-based and traceable (e.g., "DSTI 47% > 40% cap, art. 19 Reg. 17/2012"), which satisfies the Dun & Bradstreet standard better than opaque model scores.
- Synthetic data is the right choice under banking secrecy (art. 111 OUG 99/2006); any real-data pilot would need a legal basis and secrecy carve-outs.
